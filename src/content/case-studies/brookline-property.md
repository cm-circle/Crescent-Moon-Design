## The Situation

Brookline Property Group manages about 140 residential units across three buildings in the Brookline/Chestnut Hill area. Solid operation — responsive maintenance, fair rents, low turnover. But their tenant pipeline was entirely dependent on Craigslist posts and word-of-mouth from existing tenants. When a unit opened up, they'd list it, wait, and hope.

They had an Instagram account with 200 followers, last posted to eight months prior. The content was photos of empty rooms with captions like "Available now. DM for info." **Nobody was DMing.**

## What We Found

Their audience — renters aged 25-40 in the greater Boston area — lives on Instagram and increasingly TikTok. They make housing decisions based on vibe as much as square footage: what's the neighborhood like, what do the common areas feel like, are the neighbors normal, does management actually respond when something breaks? None of that was being communicated. The brand was invisible in the places where their customers were already looking.

## What We Did

- Built a content strategy around four pillars: neighborhood life (Brookline/Chestnut Hill guides, local business spotlights), tenant stories (interviews, move-in anniversaries, pet features), behind-the-scenes (maintenance day, renovation progress, team intros), and available units (styled properly, not phone photos of beige walls).
- Produced weekly content: 3 feed posts, 2 reels, daily stories. All shot on-site during monthly visits. Batch-produced so the calendar runs even between visits.
- Created a "neighborhood guide" reel series — 30-second tours of the best coffee, parks, and transit options within walking distance of each building. These consistently outperformed everything else.
- Implemented a tenant spotlight program: short interviews with long-term residents about why they stay. Built trust with prospects and made existing tenants feel valued. Win-win.
- Set up a simple lead capture: link-in-bio to a availability page, story highlights organized by building, and a DM auto-response that collects name, desired move-in date, and unit size.
- Monthly analytics reports tracking follower growth, engagement rate, saves, shares, DM leads, and — most importantly — signed leases attributed to social.

## The Result

Within four months, social media became their second-largest lead source behind only tenant referrals. They went from zero inbound DMs to averaging 38 qualified leads per month. Engagement rate jumped from 0.8% to 5.1% — well above the 1-2% industry average for property management accounts.

The neighborhood guide reels got picked up by a local Brookline lifestyle account and shared to their 12k followers. **That single share brought in 600 new followers in a weekend and filled a vacancy that had been sitting for three weeks.**

They've since expanded the retainer to include leasing-season paid promotion (targeted Instagram ads during August/September turnover) and a quarterly tenant newsletter that started as a social content repurpose.
